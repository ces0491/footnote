# Who Footnote is for

Footnote serves four groups: the production teams who decide what goes on
screen, fans watching live, fans who aren't watching live, and fans who can't
follow the pictures or the sound. This document sets out the evidence on what
each group needs, and the design principles that follow.

The evidence is uneven. Several surveys were commissioned by companies with an
interest in the result, some don't state a sample size, and much of the
accessibility research rests on small groups.

## Production teams

Stats Perform (Opta) supplies the league's official event data and, from the
2025/26 season, its "AI-powered, data-led insights"
([SVG Europe, 16 Dec 2024](https://www.svgeurope.org/blog/news-roundup/stats-perform-expands-official-media-data-partnership-with-football-dataco/)).
From 2026/27 the league produces its international coverage in-house as
Premier League Studios
([IBC365, 12 Sep 2025](https://ibc.org/production/news/building-a-true-premier-league-facility/22336)).
Its world feed has used Vizrt's Viz Engine and Viz Trio for data-driven graphics
([Vizrt, 28 Nov 2024](https://www.vizrt.com/blog/ae-live-and-vizrt/)).

**Production teams filter a lot of data down to a few stories.** At SVG
Europe's Sports Graphics Forum,
Stats Perform's Niall Hendry said "Data in isolation doesn't necessarily tell a
good story", and SailGP's Tom Peel described the job as filtering "3000-odd data
points down to something that actually tells a story"
([SVG Europe, 30 May 2025](https://www.svgeurope.org/blog/headlines/sports-graphics-forum-data-and-graphics-enhance-storytelling/)).
For the Super Bowl, Fox pre-built about 120 graphics and expected 5–10% to make
air; its Matt Gale said "the more stuff you have, the harder it's to sort
through"
([SVG, 7 Feb 2025](https://www.sportsvideo.org/2025/02/07/live-from-super-bowl-lix-fox-sports-stuffs-graphics-package-with-stats-with-assist-from-sportradar/)).

**Getting the data in is work in itself.** A freelance graphics operator at the
same forum described spending an hour before air "opening all these different
softwares just to try and get the data into our computers".

**Accuracy comes first, and the record changes.** Fox's Matt Gale: "Confirming
the accuracy of the information in all of our graphics is paramount." Official
statistics can be revised after the match: the league's Goal Accreditation
Appeals Panel moved a Brentford goal from a Harry Clarke own goal to Yoane Wissa
([premierleague.com, 29 Oct 2024](https://www.premierleague.com/news/4159875)).

**Crews keep control.** Fox Sports' David Sobel on fully automated clips: "Will
we ever go to fully automated clips, I don't think so"
([Google Cloud, 27 Aug 2026](https://cloud.google.com/transform/fox-sports-world-cup-ai-agents-future-of-sports-broadcasting)).

**Stats Perform's Niall Hendry sees personalisation as unsolved for
broadcast.** At the same forum he said: "I don't think anyone's really bridged
that gap yet on utilising AI to understand people's habits and then serving
them up."

## Fans watching live

**In two US surveys, most fans did something about the match while they
watched.** In Deloitte's March 2023 survey of 3,004 US fans aged 14 and over, 77%
did at least one game-related activity while watching at home, and 35% want
real-time stats and analytics in streams
([Deloitte, 26 Jun 2023](https://www.deloitte.com/global/en/insights/industry/sports/immersive-sports-fandom.html)).
A YouGov US article (sample size not stated) reports that 68% of soccer fans
look up stats during games, and that win probability (21%) has "a more niche
following"
([YouGov, 26 Jun 2025](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/52450-reality-checks-talkshow-rick-wolf-sports-stats)).

**In one UK survey, picture and delay mattered more than extra stats.** UK fans
in a survey by EE, a broadband and mobile provider (sample size not stated),
ranked buffering, delay and picture quality above
real-time stats, camera angles and personalised highlights
([IBC, 9 Jun 2026](https://www.ibc.org/production/news/late-night-starts-at-world-cup-to-change-uk-viewing-habits/22794)).

**Many people find offside, handball and VAR hard to follow.** In a 2021 poll
of 2,000 Britons
commissioned by Samsung, the rules people found hardest were offside (60%),
handball (46%) and when or why VAR is used (31%)
([Samsung UK, 3 Jun 2021](https://news.samsung.com/uk/var-you-sure)).

**Fans look things up in the pauses.** A 2014 study (70 questionnaires and 12
interviews) found almost half of TV football viewers seek match information the
broadcast doesn't give, mostly during pauses, before kick-off and after full
time
([Sezen, TVX2014](https://research-portal.uws.ac.uk/en/publications/analysis-of-match-related-information-seeking-behaviour-during-th/)).
In a Snapchat-commissioned survey of 1,000 UK Gen Z fans, 46% check their phone
during natural breaks and 20% while play is on
([SVG, 15 Jun 2026](https://www.sportsvideo.org/2026/06/15/fifa-world-cup-2026-mid-half-water-breaks-create-new-opportunities-to-engage-gen-z-on-smartphones/)).

**Different fans choose different feeds.** Amazon runs Prime Vision as a
separate Thursday Night Football feed for committed fans, with short "guided
viewing" segments that explain why decisions happen. Features that work there,
such as Defensive Alerts, move to the main feed
([NBC News, 10 Jan 2026](https://www.nbcnews.com/sports/nfl/amazon-wants-change-viewers-watch-nfl-rcna252479)).

**Club comes before player.** In a survey of 4,706 football fans in 121
countries (recruited on Reddit, 96.7% male), identification with the team
averaged 4.06 out of 5 and with players 3.03
([Merten et al., Sport, Business and Management, 2024](https://emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/SBM-05-2023-0063/full/html)).
In a YouGov survey of 1,005 Australians, a team was the main reason 55% of
sports fans followed a sport, against 21% for an athlete
([YouGov, 19 Jun 2025](https://yougov.com/articles/52412-more-than-half-of-australians-follow-sports-for-teams-but-younger-fans-favour-athletes)).

**Fantasy managers watch for their players.** There were 11.43 million verified
Fantasy Premier League teams at the final deadline of 2024/25
([premierleague.com, 18 Jul 2025](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4362120)).
In interviews with seven committed managers outside Europe, owning a player
decided which matches they watched
([Ahmed, 2023](https://ijmarketingsemiotics.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/ahmed_fantasy-football.pdf)).

## Fans who aren't watching live

**Stream delay causes spoilers.** In an Opinium survey of 2,000 UK adults for
Uswitch (2024), 45% had a key moment spoiled by broadcast delay, through app
alerts, news alerts, messages, social media or neighbours celebrating
([Uswitch, Jun 2024](https://www.uswitch.com/media-centre/2024/06/Match-Delay-almost-half-Brits-report-sports-spoilers-neighbours-phone-due-broadband-lag/)).
In a Big Blue Marble survey of 5,027 people across seven markets (July 2026),
62% of live sports viewers had seen a result or reaction before the action
reached their screen, 77% among Gen Z, and 35% said it spoiled the experience
([Advanced Television, 3 Sep 2026](https://www.advanced-television.com/?p=291452)).
Stats Perform says it measured delays of up to 62 seconds across streaming
providers at the 2026 Super Bowl
([SVG, 1 Jun 2026](https://www.sportsvideo.org/2026/06/01/svg-sit-down-stats-performs-charles-kaplan-on-30-years-of-opta-a-busy-summer-of-soccer-whats-next/)).

**The audience is worldwide, and some of it watches after midnight.** The
league reports 1.87 billion people following it at least weekly through the
media (method not given)
([premierleague.com, 25 May 2025](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4316617)).
In India, 27% of Premier League watch time on JioStar in 2025/26 fell between
midnight and 3am
([medianews4u, 10 Aug 2026](https://www.medianews4u.com/premier-league-clocks-more-than-9-billion-minutes-of-watch-time-on-jiostar-during-2025-26-season/)).
In GeoPoll's survey of 2,452 people aged 18–50 in five African markets, the
Premier League was the most-followed league in four of them, including South
Africa (82%)
([GeoPoll, 15 May 2026](https://www.geopoll.com/blog/football-survey-2026/)).

**Younger fans follow through clips.** In a Bango survey of Americans (sample
size not stated), 58% of 18–24s often follow games through clips, updates or
social media instead of the full match
([Bango, 16 Jun 2026](https://bango.com/51-of-gen-z-say-highlights-are-replacing-live-games/)).

**Streaming services already offer catch-up.** Peacock extended "Catch Up with
Key Plays" to soccer, basketball and golf
([TechCrunch, 20 Mar 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/03/20/peacock-takes-on-youtube-tv-and-apple-with-its-new-multiview-feature)),
Apple added Key Plays to MLS Season Pass
([World Soccer Talk, 21 Jun 2024](https://worldsoccertalk.com/tv/apple-adds-new-key-plays-feature-to-mls-season-pass/)),
and DAZN marks key moments on the progress bar with a spoiler toggle
([DAZN help](https://www.dazn.com/en-IT/help/articles/16175359846045)).

## Fans who can't follow the pictures or the sound

**TV commentary doesn't give blind and partially sighted fans enough detail to
follow the match.** Ten fans
interviewed by RNIB rated TV football 6.1 out of 15 because commentators "do not
provide enough description or detail for the match to be followed accurately".
They wanted to know where the ball and players were, the type of pass and the
build-up to goals, and disliked information "provided after the event"
([RNIB, Nov 2022](https://media.rnib.org.uk/documents/RNIB_report_on_accessibility_of_TV_football_broadcasts.pdf)).
In RNIB focus groups, some found audio description "too frantic and
overwhelming", one noted that nothing describes the offside lines during a VAR
check, and one fan's wish was an app with "stats on one side which you can
touch, and it starts reading them"
([RNIB focus group 1](https://media.rnib.org.uk/documents/RNIB_first_focus_group_on_accessibility_of_football_broadcasts_2022.pdf),
[focus group 2](https://media.rnib.org.uk/documents/RNIB_second_focus_group_on_accessibility_of_football_broadcasts_2022.pdf)).
RNIB's guidance: "Text on screen must be read out"
([RNIB, Aug 2023](https://media.rnib.org.uk/documents/Best_practice_for_making_sports_commentaries_accessible_for_viewers_with_visio_xuVYOS9.pdf)).

**Provision is new and partial.** From 10 October 2026 Sky added live
descriptive commentary to about 109 Premier League matches a season and live
subtitles to about 210, tested with RNIB
([Diageo, 8 Oct 2026](https://www.diageo.com/en/news-and-media/press-releases/2026/sky-media-and-guinness-launch-sky-sports-biggest-expansion);
[SVG Europe, 13 Oct 2026](https://www.svgeurope.org/blog/headlines/sky-sports-boosts-accessibility-offering-with-live-description-and-audio-commentary-for-premier-league-coverage/)).
A season has 380 matches.

**Shorter captions with less delay scored better in one study.** In a Toronto
study of 27 deaf and hard-of-hearing viewers watching hockey and basketball,
captioning only the colour commentary (about 2 s delay) scored higher than full
play-by-play (about 4 s), whose captions "interfere with watching the gameplay",
though most of those asked (17 of 26) reported no preference
([Nam et al., 19 Apr 2023](https://webs.uab.cat/livesubtitling/wp-content/uploads/sites/209/2023/06/LiveSubtitling_Conference_NAM_somang.pdf)).
A survey of 216 US deaf and hard-of-hearing viewers found caption latency
"significantly" affects the experience
([Thompson et al., arXiv, Sep 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11408)).

**Lights and noise overload some fans.** Research on match-day visits with 16
neurodivergent fans and 8 carers or guardians found sensory overload triggered
by "bright or flashing lights, loud and unexpected noises", as well as strong
smells and crowds, and announcements shown as text on stadium screens among the
things that helped
([NatCen for SGSA, Jul 2021](https://sgsa.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/Neurodiverse-sports-fans.pdf)).
On screens, WCAG requires a way to pause, stop or hide moving content that
starts by itself, lasts more than five seconds and runs alongside other content
([SC 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html))
and allows nothing to flash more than three times in any one second unless the
flashes stay below its thresholds
([SC 2.3.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html)).

**Regulation is moving towards streamers.** Ofcom's draft code for large UK
streaming services proposes audio description on 2.5% of content rising to 10%
by year four, with no genre automatically exempt
([Wiggin, 2026](https://wiggin.co.uk/insight/two-significant-ofcom-consultation-documents-published-as-part-of-the-implementation-of-the-media-act/)).
In France, since 28 June 2025, the law has required services giving access to
audiovisual media to pass on in full the access services that exist,
"synchronized with sound and video", and to let users adjust them, with Arcom
overseeing it
([Arcom](https://www.arcom.fr/en/we-know/our-missions/ensuring-plurality-and-social-cohesion/accessibility-of-services-giving-access-to-audiovisual-media-services)).

**Mistakes matter more to fans who can't see the screen.** A described event
that didn't happen misleads someone who can't check it by looking.

**Users should shape the service.** RNIB's Jonny Marshall said these services
work best when "the people who use these services are involved in shaping them"
(Diageo, above).

## What has gone wrong before

- IBM's 2024 Wimbledon player stories called Emma Raducanu the British No. 1
  when she was third in Britain's rankings, and described two players aged 27
  and 35 as up-and-comers
  ([Consultancy.uk, 5 Jul 2024](https://www.consultancy.uk/news/37705/wimbledon-ai-app-mocked-for-unforced-errors)).
- ESPN's AI recap of Alex Morgan's final match didn't mention her, though ESPN
  had said a human editor would review each AI recap
  ([Awful Announcing, 9 Sep 2024](https://awfulannouncing.com/espn/alex-morgan-ai-generated-recap-mention.html)).
- AI-cloned commentator voices on PDC darts were called "robotic and unnatural"
  ([dartsnews.com, 29 Jul 2025](https://dartsnews.com/pdc/ai-commentary-on-pdc-tv-raises-eyebrows-it-lacks-emotion-and-nuance)).
- Nickelodeon's NFL altcast fell from over 2 million viewers at its 2021 debut
  to between 0.9 and 1.3 million in its later games
  ([Awful Announcing, 27 Dec 2024](https://awfulannouncing.com/tv/officially-reached-altcast-oversaturation.html)).

## Design principles

1. **Pick the story.** Rank candidate insights and show a few, each with its
   reason. *(Production teams)*
2. **Code computes, the model phrases.** Every number comes from code, each
   sentence cites the events it rests on or a fact code computed, and an amended
   event flags every insight that cited it. *(Production teams; what has gone
   wrong before)*
3. **The producer decides what airs.** *(Production teams)*
4. **Never ahead of the viewer's screen.** Each insight is released when that
   viewer's playback reaches the moment. *(Fans who aren't watching live)*
5. **Explain in the pauses.** Keep live play uncluttered and explain rule calls
   when they happen. *(Fans watching live)*
6. **A mode for each kind of fan.** Casual and analyst are distinct
   experiences; player focus sits on top of the fan's club. *(Fans watching live)*
7. **Catch-up for anyone who missed it.** *(Fans who aren't watching live)*
8. **Every graphic has a text equivalent.** The viewer controls detail, pace and
   motion. *(Fans who can't follow the pictures or the sound)*
